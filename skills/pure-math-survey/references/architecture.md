# Architecture and mathematical hierarchy

Plan from the requested reading task, not from a catalog of papers or files. The architecture note records the central question, audience, selected scope, major sections and their connections. It also records the core routes and selected explanation targets when III is requested. This is one editorial note, not an additional audit database or a mandatory printed chart.

## Three logical levels

**Mathematical perspective.** State what the article asks, why it matters and which larger questions or relationships organize the answer. A short article can have one thread. Functional labels such as background, results and methods do not by themselves identify the mathematical thread.

**Major sections.** Make each section a principal unit of that argument. Explain what it establishes, why it belongs and how it connects to the preceding and following units. Group related local results under a larger mathematical task. An independently stored component is not thereby a top-level section.

**Local subsections.** Put technical variants, calculations, examples and subsidiary claims beneath the task they support. Keep genuinely independent major questions as sections. Do not create decorative levels or prescribe a number of sections, themes, paragraphs or headings.

Write the introduction in this logical order: concrete central problem and significance, organizing mathematical perspective and thematic relationships, then section-level roles and connections. Use real section references or meaningful groups, not a recital of every subsection. Introduce only the notation needed to understand the central problem. The abstract summarizes content and perspective; the introduction explains organization; the contents locates material. None replaces the other two.

Reorganize the body at the same time. Each section opens with a short transition explaining its present task and relevant local development without mechanically repeating the introduction. Concise and standard may have different printed hierarchies. `\input` boundaries are implementation details. After drafting, read only the introduction and major section titles: can a reviewer reconstruct the central question, larger threads, relationships and section duties? Then verify those promises in the body and check local references. Keywords or title counts cannot establish success.

## Part-specific organization

| Part | Mathematical thread in the introduction | Relationship between major sections |
|---|---|---|
| I | Why the objects and questions matter; what a model reveals | The necessary language enables a model, whose structure leads to the general question |
| II | How known conclusions answer the central question; which distinctions matter | Result groups organized by implication, comparison, difference or improvement |
| III | Which large proof ideas resolve which difficulties | Method families divide the work; local bridges serve a route; the outputs close the target |
| IV | Where the theory applies, where it is limited, and what gaps define the frontier | Applications and boundaries lead to Problem groups; progress changes their remaining scope |
| V | What is known overall, why the methods help, and what deserves further study | Necessary language supports selected results and their relationships; method ideas and Problems complete the thematic account |

## I: Foundations and Models

Explain the central objects and a completed core model, construction or tractable case, then show what transfers to the general problem. Supply the definitions and background actually used. Standard deepens foundations and model reasoning; concise preserves the necessary conceptual bridge. Do not require a governing equation or invent an obstacle where the model's reasoning already extends directly. A model here explains an object or question; an application later tests the force of a result.

## II: Results and Relations

Organize core results by the mathematical questions they answer, not author chronology. State complete hypotheses and conclusions, cite controlling versions and explain the results' relationship. Match objects, assumptions, quantifiers, ranges and conclusions before claiming implication, improvement or equivalence. Prove or cite review-derived comparisons. Give substantive proof ideas at this part's scale; detailed development belongs to III when requested. A concise explanation must not defer all meaning to standard.

## III: Proofs and Methods

Explain each promised core proof family's target, main issue, decisive idea, intermediate outputs, uses of hypotheses and closing inference. Separately develop selected decisive mechanisms: a genuine bottleneck, short bridge or transferable construction can qualify. Standard deepens these arguments and dependencies where helpful. Precisely stated and located background inputs need not be reproved. Record what is imported and what this document promises to explain; do not relabel an unfulfilled central target as background. Review the original architecture against both requested III entries, including their actual input files.

## IV: Applications, Boundaries and Problems

Use the theory through meaningful constructions or applications with checked hypotheses and a derived conclusion. Distinguish failure of an assumption, failure of a method and failure of a conclusion. A counterexample is appropriate only when established, not as a required category. Organize the agreed core frontier through explicit Problem statements, then discuss verified progress, remaining ranges, importance and evidenced relationships. Standard adds depth to these applications and discussions; concise retains the core Problems, not merely a promise that they appear elsewhere.

## V: An independently organized thematic review

Choose V's own central question and core. Use verified research and, where helpful, other parts' exposition; neither I–IV's prior existence nor a particular generation order is required. The common core of V's editions is not the union of all I–IV nodes.

Explain the problem's background and importance, necessary objects and notation, accurate main conclusions and their relationships, ideas behind the methods, and selected worthwhile Problems or directions. Independent readability means that the chosen reader can understand these statements and conditions within V. It does not mean teaching an entire prerequisite course or reproducing I's models.

For concise, select principal results and necessary support, merge repeated explanations, combine local sections and drop secondary calculations or branches. Explain a method's idea, job and connection to the conclusion, usually in a compact paragraph. Retain a short derivation only when it materially clarifies the thread. Do not automatically import III's complete proof families or technical lemma inventory. Do not shrink fonts or margins or erase conditions to obtain brevity.

Standard deepens background relationships, comparisons, key method ideas and the meaning and progress of the same core Problems. Useful short derivations or examples may be added, but detailed proof obligations arise only from the actual request and declared promises, not V's part number.

The two V templates give a complete editable starting skeleton: Introduction; Setting and notation; Main results and their relations; Ideas behind the results; Problems and further directions. These are functional prompts, not a fixed five-section taxonomy. Replace them with mathematical titles and, where clearer, interweave results and method ideas. Each template's introduction asks for the actual thematic relationships and section references. Concise includes its own selection and merging decisions; standard specifies where deeper understanding is added.

Give V one abstract, introduction, notation system, contents, numbering and bibliography. Reuse canonical full statements once, with valid local references afterward; reuse, compress, rewrite or reorganize prose freely. A reference to an unproduced companion must not carry necessary assumptions. Reuse does not certify integration quality, imply a generation order or reveal token consumption.

## Shared facts and different selections

A mapped node has one mathematical body and a part I–IV owner describing its role, even when it appears only in V. Ownership creates no obligation to produce that standalone part. V's two editions may independently give a full statement, an accurately located and self-sufficient reference, or a justified omission outside their core. I–IV standard retains its full-statement rule. Use the [format reference](records-and-delivery.md) rather than expanding the schema.

Shared statements and Problems retain their assumptions, source versions and knowledge status. A V Problem selection need not exhaust IV; it must satisfy V's own advertised thread without implying omitted subjects are settled. Repair shared facts once and check actual affected placements. Core selection and reasons for omission require substantive review, not automated coverage scores.
