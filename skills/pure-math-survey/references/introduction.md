# Introduction: a main-conclusion overview

The Introduction must let the intended reader acquire the article's principal mathematical knowledge without reading the body first. This applies to all five parts, both editions, and single-article updates. It is separate from the abstract, contents, historical motivation, and investigation of core Problems.

## What the reader must be able to recover

After reading only the Introduction, the reader should know the actual principal conclusions, the objects and assumptions to which they apply, their essential parameter ranges, and important limitations. They should be able to distinguish an equivalence from a one-way implication, a special-model correspondence from a general theorem, a smooth solution from a weak limit, and an established conclusion from a conjectural target when those distinctions affect the article.

The overview must contain mathematical statements, not only names or locations. “We discuss existence and stability” gives no answer. “See Theorem 4.2” gives a location, not the conclusion. A list of sections, authors, or topics is still not the overview. Nor does one displayed theorem automatically represent an article whose body develops several independent main answers.

## Select the principal results, then cover them

Determine the selected manuscript's main-result set from its actual purpose and body. A principal result answers an organizing question, provides a major criterion or classification, supplies a central construction, or changes the valid interpretation through an important obstruction or counterexample. An auxiliary estimate is not automatically principal; a short decisive counterexample can be.

For each principal result, provide a substantive introductory statement. A group of closely related results can be combined when the mathematics genuinely forms one equivalence or coherent classification. Do not mix unrelated consequences into a swollen theorem. Do not demote a promised main conclusion after drafting simply because adding it to the Introduction is inconvenient.

There is no universal number of results. A narrow article with one principal result can have one. A broad article with multiple main conclusions must cover that set, not choose a single convenient “highlight.” Do not enforce the number of results used in the retained v6 example on another topic.

Technical lemmas, routine immediate consequences, and material genuinely outside the selected principal scope need not all appear in the Introduction. The requirement is complete coverage of the article's principal answers, not duplication of every formal environment.

## The content of one overview entry

An entry needs the following information to the extent material to its meaning:

- Objects and fixed data, with the unknown or class of objects being classified.
- Essential assumptions, quantifiers, branch or parameter range, and relevant equivalence relation.
- The actual conclusion, preferably in precise formulas where clearer.
- Source and controlling version qualifications, together with an internal location for explanation or proof.

A forward reference supplements a statement; it cannot replace it. Define needed local notation before use. Do not leave a decisive stability notion, functional, norm, category, or positivity condition undefined only to save lines. A short local definition or repeated setting can be the cheapest route to clarity.

Self-contained does not require reproducing standard prerequisites for the intended reader. It does require enough information to understand what the statement says and when it applies. If a broad programme cannot be defined at the chosen length, identify its limited discussion as such, separately from the formal established conclusions.

## Order and typography

Use the minimum setup needed to understand the first conclusions, then list the main statements early. Follow mathematical dependencies: data, relevant identity or structure, equation or question, and results. Do not place a long historical tour or a full proof before the main-conclusion overview merely to avoid later repetition.

Numbered theorem-like statements, a precise numbered result list, or short thematic subsections containing those statements are acceptable. The printed heading need not literally be “Main conclusions.” Functional template headings should be renamed for the mathematics while preserving the role.

Do not invent optional theorem titles. Use actual authors and citations where justified. Keep hypotheses in readable `itemize` or `enumerate` lists with their logic explicit. Keep unified equivalences intact; put genuine secondary consequences afterward. Delete transitions that add no information. A coherent sequence of definitions and theorems does not need narrative filler.

Use the same typography as the body. Do not compress the Introduction with smaller type, tighter margins, or shrunken displays. Repair short statement breaks locally when they impede scanning; a longer statement can cross pages naturally. There is no fixed introductory page quota.

## Important negative information

The overview must not imply a stronger headline result than the body proves. Include a major counterexample, failed converse, restricted range, or need for additional hypotheses when its omission would produce that impression. For instance, a criterion for existence is not automatically a theorem of convergence from every initial datum. A sufficient special-case condition is not a necessary-and-sufficient unrestricted classification.

Place unresolved targets and conjectural programmes in visibly distinct text or Problem statements. Do not count an open question as an established answer. Preserve the source's verification status; an editorial rearrangement does not establish an updated literature cutoff.

## Part-specific application

| Part | What its introductory overview must convey |
|---|---|
| I | The substantive model/construction conclusions and the justified connection to the general problem, not only definitions |
| II | The selected main theorems, criteria, classifications, and genuine relations between them |
| III | The conclusions obtained by the principal proof routes under their decisive hypotheses, not merely technique names |
| IV | The significant application/progress/boundary conclusions; unresolved targets are separately marked |
| V | The independent review's principal answers across its selected strands, not the entire I–IV inventory |

Both concise and standard editions meet this requirement for their own actual selected content. Concise may have a narrower scope, but it cannot hide its body conclusions behind references to standard. Standard may deepen explanations without postponing the answers. The requirement does not create unrequested companion volumes or complete-proof obligations.

## Two manual acceptance checks

**Introduction-only reading.** Temporarily ignore the body. Write down the mathematical conclusions that can actually be learned, along with their necessary conditions and main limitations. If the answer is mostly topic names or pointers, revise the overview. Check definitions and any formula used to compress a statement.

**Body-to-Introduction coverage.** Traverse the principal body results and locate the corresponding introductory statements. Compare objects, hypotheses, quantifiers, ranges, implications/equivalences, conclusions, limitations, and source status. Missing coverage or a changed logical force is a substantive defect. A correct canonical theorem body does not automatically validate the prose summary around it.

Use the existing architecture note and release evidence to record locations and repairs. Do not create a new coverage CSV, numerical score, theorem quota, or token budget. Static tests can ensure that templates demonstrate an overview and that sample pointers are correct. They cannot judge whether the overview contains the right mathematics or covers the principal answers.

## Precise special cases and synchronized revision

When the full theorem requires disproportionate specialized language, an explicitly limited exact special case may represent the selected main knowledge. Identify the general theorem and state which range is omitted. A range central to the article's promise cannot be omitted this way. Selected results cannot be retroactively demoted to avoid coverage.

Adding, merging or removing a principal result, changing its assumptions or source version, or discovering a claimed resolution triggers a linked recheck of the introduction, abstract, body, canonical statements and architecture mapping. A fresh preprint remains qualified at each material use.

Apply the length review triggers in length-and-selection.md. A short Part I–IV does not need to repeat all the results of Part V. If its introduction cannot fit without omitting essential conditions, reconsider that document's scope before writing more pages.
